7 Warning Signs Your Consumer Unit Needs Upgrading

Warning signs that a consumer unit needs upgrading include an old board with rewireable fuses, no RCD protection, frequent tripping, a burning smell or scorch marks, buzzing, and a board that is full with no spare ways. If you notice any of these in a Dorset home, have it inspected. A modern consumer unit improves shock and fire protection.

Your consumer unit, often called the fuse board, is the heart of your home's electrical system. It controls the power to every circuit and, on a modern board, protects you against electric shock and fire. Most of the time it sits quietly out of the way and gets forgotten about. The trouble is that an older or failing board can put your home at risk without any obvious sign, so it pays to know what to look for. Here are seven warning signs that your consumer unit may be due an upgrade.

1. An old rewireable fuse box

If your board has old style rewireable fuses, where you replace a piece of fuse wire when something blows, rather than switches you flick back on, it is a good few decades out of date. These boards do not offer the level of protection modern circuit breakers and RCDs provide, and a fuse rewired with the wrong gauge of wire can be dangerous. A modern consumer unit with MCBs or RCBOs is a significant safety improvement over a fuse box of this age.

2. No RCD protection

An RCD, or residual current device, cuts the power in a fraction of a second if it detects a fault such as electricity flowing to earth through a person. It is one of the most important safety features in a home, and older boards often have none at all. If there is no test button on your board, or an electrician confirms there is no RCD protection, an upgrade is well worth considering. It is the single biggest factor in protecting your household from electric shock.

3. Frequent tripping

The occasional trip is normal and shows the protection is doing its job. Frequent, repeated tripping is not. It can point to an overloaded circuit, a faulty appliance, or deterioration in the wiring or the board itself. Rather than simply resetting the switch again and again, it is worth having the cause investigated. Sometimes the fix is straightforward, but persistent tripping on an older board is often a sign it is reaching the end of its useful life.

4. A burning smell or scorch marks

A smell of burning plastic near the board, or scorch marks and discolouration around the fuses or switches, should never be ignored. These are signs of overheating, loose connections, or arcing, any of which can lead to a fire. If you notice this, switch off at the main switch if it is safe to do so and call an electrician straight away. This is one warning sign that needs attention urgently rather than at your convenience.

5. Buzzing or crackling

A healthy consumer unit is silent. Buzzing, humming or crackling coming from the board usually means a loose connection or a component that is failing. As with scorch marks, these noises can be an early warning of overheating. It is not something to live with. Have it inspected promptly, because a small fault caught early is far cheaper and safer to deal with than the damage it can cause if it is left.

6. The board is full with no spare ways

If every slot on your board is used and there is no room to add a new circuit, you are limited whenever you want to extend or improve your home. Adding an outdoor supply, a shower, an electric vehicle charger or a kitchen circuit all need space on the board. A full board is not dangerous in itself, but upgrading to a larger, modern unit at the same time as adding protection is often the sensible, cost effective choice.

7. Old rubber or fabric wiring feeding it

If the cables feeding or leaving your board have rubber or fabric insulation rather than modern PVC, the installation is likely to be very old and the insulation may be perished. Deteriorating wiring is a real fire and shock risk, and an old board usually goes hand in hand with it. In this situation an electrician may recommend an inspection to assess the whole installation, as the wiring itself, not just the board, may need attention.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a new consumer unit cost?

Consumer unit upgrades typically start at around £1,000 plus VAT, though the exact price depends on your existing setup and the size of the property. We always provide a free, no obligation quote so you know the full cost before we start.

How long does a consumer unit upgrade take?

Most upgrades take around four to six hours, depending on the size of the property and the condition of the existing wiring. We keep disruption to a minimum, test everything, and talk you through what has changed before we leave.

Is it dangerous to keep an old fuse board?

An old board is not always an immediate danger, but it usually offers less protection than a modern unit, particularly against electric shock and fire. If you notice any of the warning signs above, it is worth having it inspected so you can make an informed decision.

Will the upgrade be certified?

Yes. Every consumer unit we install is fully tested and certified, so you have written proof the work meets current standards. This is useful for your own peace of mind, and for insurers or future buyers.
