10 Questions to Ask Your Electrician in Poole Before Hiring

Before hiring an electrician in Poole, check they are registered with a scheme such as NICEIC, ask whether they are insured, confirm the work will be certified, and get a clear written quote. This guide covers ten questions that help you tell a reliable, qualified electrician from a risky one, and explains what a good answer sounds like.

Choosing an electrician is not just about who gives the lowest price. Electrical work is safety critical, and poor work can put your home and family at risk long after the invoice is paid. A little time spent asking the right questions before you hire tells you a great deal about who you are dealing with. Below are ten questions worth asking any electrician in Poole or the wider Dorset area, along with what a good answer sounds like and why it matters.

1. Are you registered with a competent person scheme?

Ask whether the electrician is registered with a recognised scheme such as NICEIC or NAPIT. Registered contractors are assessed against national safety standards on a regular basis, so their work is independently checked rather than simply taken on trust. It also means they can self certify notifiable work with your local authority, saving you the cost and delay of a separate building control application. If someone cannot name a scheme or dodges the question, treat that as a warning sign.

2. Are you insured?

A reliable electrician will carry public liability insurance, which covers damage to your property or injury caused while they are working. If an uninsured trader damages your home, you may have no way to recover the cost. There is nothing wrong with asking to see a copy of the cover, and a professional will be happy to provide it. It is a simple check that protects you if something goes wrong.

3. Will the work be certified, and will I get the certificate?

Most installation and inspection work should be tested and certified on completion, whether that is an Electrical Installation Certificate, a Minor Works Certificate, or an EICR. The certificate is your written proof that the work meets the current wiring regulations. You will need it for insurers, for future buyers, and for your own peace of mind. Confirm up front that a certificate is included in the price, not charged as an extra afterwards.

4. Will you give me a written quote?

A verbal estimate is easy to forget and easy to dispute. A written quote sets out what is included, what is not, and the price you are agreeing to. It lets you compare electricians fairly and avoids nasty surprises when the bill arrives. At 2 Sparks Electrical we provide free, no obligation written quotes so you know exactly what to expect before any work begins. Be cautious of anyone who will not put a price in writing.

5. How much experience do you have?

Experience counts for a lot in electrical work, especially with fault finding and older properties where nothing is ever quite standard. Ask how long they have been trading and what type of work they handle most. You are looking for someone who has seen a wide range of jobs and can explain their approach clearly. 6. Is this work notifiable under Part P?

Part P of the Building Regulations covers electrical safety in homes. Certain jobs, such as a new consumer unit or new circuits, are notifiable, meaning building control has to be told. A scheme registered electrician can notify the work themselves and issue you a compliance certificate. A good electrician will know straight away whether your job is notifiable and will handle it for you. If they seem unsure what Part P is, that tells you something.

7. Can you provide references or reviews?

Genuine tradespeople are usually happy to point you to reviews or previous customers. Recent, verifiable feedback is a good sign that other people in your area have had a positive experience. Look for consistency rather than a single glowing comment, and take note of how the electrician responds to any less than perfect feedback. Word of mouth and local reputation matter, so ask around as well as reading online.

8. Do you guarantee your work?

Ask whether the electrician stands behind their work if a problem appears later. A confident tradesperson who takes pride in the job will be happy to put things right if their workmanship is at fault. Combined with certification and scheme registration, a clear position on guarantees shows you are dealing with someone who intends to do the job properly the first time rather than move on and forget about it.

9. What are the timescales?

Before you commit, get a realistic idea of when the work can start and how long it will take. This matters most for larger jobs like a rewire, where the disruption needs planning around your life. A dependable electrician will give you honest timescales rather than promising the earth to win the job. Clear communication at this stage is usually a good indicator of how the whole project will be handled.

10. What are the payment terms?

Understand how and when you are expected to pay. For larger projects a staged payment is normal, but be wary of anyone demanding a large sum up front in cash before any work has started. Reasonable, clearly stated terms are a sign of an established business. Make sure you know whether prices include VAT and what happens if extra work is needed once the job is under way.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check an electrician is NICEIC registered?

You can search the official NICEIC contractor directory online to confirm an electrician is genuinely registered. Ask for their registration details and check them yourself rather than relying on a logo on a van or website.

Should an electrician always give a certificate?

Yes, for installation and inspection work you should receive the relevant certificate on completion. It confirms the work meets the current wiring regulations and is useful for insurers and future buyers, so keep it safe.

Is the cheapest quote usually the best choice?

Not always. A very low price can mean corners are being cut on materials, testing or certification. Compare what is actually included in each written quote, and weigh price against scheme registration, insurance and experience.
